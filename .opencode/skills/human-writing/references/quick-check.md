# Quick Check: The One-Page Cheatsheet

Use this for fast rewrites when you don't need the full reference files. Scan the piece against these checks. Fix the ones that fail.

---

## The 30-second scan

Before rewriting, look for:

- [ ] **Opener is generic.** "In today's…", "In recent years…", "Since the dawn of…" → rewrite the first sentence entirely
- [ ] **Closer is AI-templated.** "In conclusion…", "Ultimately…", "As we move forward…" → delete the closer or replace with something specific
- [ ] **AI vocab density.** Count these in the piece: delve, leverage, robust, seamless, holistic, navigate, foster, multifaceted, crucial, pivotal, comprehensive, harness, empower, cutting-edge, underscore, testament, realm, landscape, bolster, unpack, spearhead, transformative, paradigm, vibrant, pave the way. More than 2 → cut aggressively
- [ ] **Em-dash density.** Count em-dashes, divide by sentence count. More than 1 per 4 sentences → cut most of them. Currently the #1 punctuation tell of 2026.
- [ ] **"Not X — Y" patterns.** "Not just a tool, a revolution." LinkedIn-weaponized. Cut on sight unless the contrast is genuinely load-bearing.
- [ ] **Padding phrases.** "It is important to note", "It's worth mentioning", "When it comes to", "In terms of", "With regard to" → delete every instance
- [ ] **No specifics.** Any names, numbers, dates, brands, places in the piece? If no → add some or flag for the user
- [ ] **Flat sentence lengths.** If every sentence is between 15 and 25 words → add a short one (<8 words) and a long one (>30)
- [ ] **Every paragraph same size.** If paragraphs all hover around 4 sentences → merge some, split others
- [ ] **No opinion visible.** Could this have been written by anyone? → add a stance
- [ ] **Tricolon count.** "X, Y, and Z" lists linked by commas — more than 2 in the piece means cut one

## The burstiness fix

If sentences feel flat: within each paragraph, guarantee at least one sentence under 8 words and one over 30.

Fast techniques:
- **Break a medium sentence into two:** "The plan is ambitious and faces several hurdles." → "The plan's ambitious. And it faces several hurdles."
- **Join two mediums into a long one:** Use em dashes, semicolons, or "and" without a comma
- **Add a fragment:** "We tried it. It didn't work. Not even close."

---

## The voice injection

If the piece is voiceless, add one of these in the first paragraph:

- A blunt opinion: "Honestly, X is overrated."
- A contrarian hook: "Everyone says X. They're wrong."
- A specific hate or love: "I hate [specific thing]. Here's why it matters."
- A confession: "I used to think X. I was wrong."
- A question that commits: "Why does everyone pretend X? Let's be real."

One voice marker per 100 words is a good baseline.

---

## The specificity fix

For every generic noun phrase, try to swap in a specific one:

- "many companies" → "Boeing, Ford, and GE"
- "recent years" → "since around 2021"
- "significant growth" → "up 34% since Q2"
- "various studies" → "a Stanford paper from 2022"
- "some experts" → "[name a person]"

If you don't have the specific, invent a plausible one *only* when context allows creative license (marketing, personal essays, opinion writing). For factual/technical/academic text, flag the gap to the user instead of fabricating.

---

## Red flags that mean "start over, don't just edit"

Sometimes a piece is so thoroughly AI-shaped that surgical edits won't save it. Start from scratch when:

- The structure is a perfect five-paragraph essay
- Every paragraph starts with a topic sentence
- There are zero specifics and the topic is one where specifics exist
- The whole piece could be summarized as "X is important and multifaceted"
- Every transition uses "Moreover," or "Furthermore,"
- The piece hedges every claim to the point of saying nothing
- Reads like LinkedIn or a press release regardless of the stated register

In these cases, extract the one actual point and rewrite around it.

---

## Final check before delivering

Read the piece once more, aloud in your head. Ask:

1. Does this sound like a person talking?
2. Is there anything surprising — a word, a turn of phrase, a specific reference?
3. Would I bother reading this to the end if a friend sent it to me?

If no to any of these, one more pass.
