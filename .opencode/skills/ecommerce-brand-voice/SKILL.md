# eCommerce Brand Voice

Inspired by:
amanamabasiakpan/Claude-Skills-for-eCommerce

## Purpose

Help AI write consistently for a business
without resorting to generic marketing language.

Use this skill for:
- Brand voice development
- Product descriptions
- Category and collection content
- Homepage messaging
- Email marketing
- Social media content
- Brand storytelling
- Existing marketing copy reviews

Applicable to retail, wholesale, B2B, DTC,
and inquiry-based commerce.

Never assume the business has online checkout.

## 1. Discover existing brand context

Before drafting content, inspect available:
- Project documentation
- Existing website copy
- Approved brand guidelines
- Product catalog information
- Marketing examples
- Customer communication examples

Check for a project-specific brand guide at:
- .opencode/brand-guide.md
- .claude/brand-guide.md

Use available guidance before asking
for information already documented.

Never substitute invented brand details
for missing information.

## 2. Understand the business

Identify:
- Business name and positioning
- Products or services offered
- Target customers
- Market segment
- Important differentiators
- Typical customer journey
- Primary conversion goal

A conversion goal may be:
- Purchase
- Product inquiry
- Quote request
- Appointment
- Sample request
- Contact submission

Match writing to the actual business model.

## 3. Define brand personality

Establish the intended characteristics:
- Formal or conversational
- Technical or approachable
- Contemporary or traditional
- Premium or value-focused
- Reserved or expressive
- Educational or promotional

Avoid contradictory tones.

Maintain consistency without making
every sentence sound identical.

## 4. Understand customer language

Identify:
- What customers are trying to achieve
- Questions they commonly ask
- Their evaluation criteria
- Common objections
- Their familiarity with the products
- Words and descriptions they actually use

Use verified reviews, inquiries, and
customer conversations when authorized.

Do not fabricate customer quotations
or represent imagined personas as research.

## 5. Build a reusable brand guide

When asked, create or revise a guide containing:

### Brand
- Identity and positioning
- Audience
- Products and services
- Differentiators

### Voice
- Tone characteristics
- Preferred vocabulary
- Words and claims to avoid
- Example writing samples

### Messaging
- Core value proposition
- Customer priorities
- Supported proof points
- Calls to action

### Commerce
- Sales model
- Product information standards
- Typical customer journey
- Conversion objectives

Keep the guide concise and actionable.

Store it in the relevant project only
after user approval.

Never place private business information
in the public starter repository.

## 6. Apply the voice consistently

For each writing task:

1. Identify the audience and channel.
2. Read applicable brand guidance.
3. Identify the intended message.
4. Confirm relevant factual details.
5. Draft in the established voice.
6. Review for tone consistency.
7. Check accuracy and readability.

Different channels may require
different levels of detail or formality.

Maintain the same underlying identity.

## 7. Product content standards

For product and category writing:
- Preserve verified product attributes.
- Use industry-appropriate terminology.
- Explain meaningful distinctions.
- Avoid unsupported claims of quality.
- Avoid repetitive template language.
- Match customer knowledge level.
- Respect catalog naming conventions.

Do not invent:
- Materials
- Dimensions
- Manufacturing locations
- Certifications
- Availability
- Performance claims

## 9. Review criteria

Before completing a writing task, confirm:
- The text sounds like the intended brand.
- The customer is clearly understood.
- Product and business facts are accurate.
- The language suits the channel.
- Claims have supporting evidence.
- The call to action fits the business model.
- The text does not sound generic or robotic.

## Boundaries

Do not invent facts, endorsements,
customer experiences, or brand history.

Do not overwrite existing brand guidance
without approval.

Follow the Planner approval process
for changes to project files.

Do not publish content without authorization.
