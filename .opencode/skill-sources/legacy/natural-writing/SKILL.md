# Natural Writing

Inspired by blader/humanizer and
amineall/human-writing-skills.

## Purpose

Create writing that sounds natural, specific, and useful.

Apply to:
- Website content
- Product descriptions
- Blog posts and articles
- Emails and marketing copy
- Brand messaging
- Existing drafts requiring revision

Do not apply stylistic rewriting to source code,
structured data, or technical specifications unless asked.

## 1. Understand the audience

Before writing, identify:

- Who will read the content
- What they want to know
- The intended publication channel
- The desired tone
- The required information
- Existing brand voice guidance

Use supplied context before asking questions.

## 2. Match the voice

When writing samples are provided:

- Study vocabulary and sentence rhythm.
- Match the formality and personality.
- Preserve meaningful stylistic preferences.
- Avoid mechanically copying individual phrases.

Without examples, use clear and conversational prose
appropriate to the audience.

Professional writing does not have to sound corporate.

## 3. Write naturally

Prefer:

- Direct statements
- Specific information
- Natural sentence variation
- Useful examples
- Clear paragraph progression
- Precise vocabulary
- Appropriate contractions
- An authentic, context-appropriate tone

Avoid:

- Generic motivational language
- Repetitive sentence structures
- Forced three-part lists
- Artificially dramatic conclusions
- Excessive em dashes
- Unnecessary introductory phrases
- Unsupported superlatives
- Exaggerated marketing claims
- Decorative formatting without purpose

Do not make prose intentionally awkward to mimic humans.

## 4. Preserve factual accuracy

Never invent:

- Customer testimonials
- Product specifications
- Materials or manufacturing origins
- Certifications or awards
- Sales figures
- Research findings
- Personal experiences
- Quotations or sources

When information is missing, omit the unsupported
claim or request clarification.

Creative fiction is an exception when explicitly requested.

## 5. Write useful first drafts

Do not always generate generic prose and then
perform a separate humanization pass.

Instead:

1. Establish the intended message.
2. Choose an appropriate voice.
3. Draft naturally from the beginning.
4. Perform one focused editorial review.
5. Deliver the finished text.

Avoid unnecessary rewrite iterations.

## 6. Humanize existing drafts

For supplied text:

1. Read the entire original.
2. Identify repetitive or artificial phrasing.
3. Preserve all supported facts and intended meaning.
4. Rewrite paragraphs where necessary.
5. Check for lost or added claims.
6. Return the improved draft.

Retain the author's genuine opinions and personality.

## 7. SEO compatibility

If SEO requirements exist:

- Preserve search intent.
- Include relevant terminology naturally.
- Prioritize readability and usefulness.
- Maintain accurate product and business information.
- Avoid keyword stuffing.
- Preserve verified internal links.

SEO never justifies inaccurate claims.

Use the relevant SEO skill for detailed optimization.

## 8. Multilingual writing

Write in the requested language.

Respect the language's natural grammar,
punctuation, tone, and regional conventions.

Do not translate English marketing clichés literally.

## 9. Final quality check

Before delivery, verify:

- The intended message is clear.
- The voice suits the audience.
- Every factual claim is supported.
- Repetition and filler are minimized.
- No important information was lost.
- The text sounds natural when read aloud.
- The content remains useful and accurate.

## Boundaries

Do not invent factual details for authenticity.
Do not claim content was written by a human.
Do not promise AI-detector evasion.
Do not publish or modify approved content without
the appropriate user authorization.
