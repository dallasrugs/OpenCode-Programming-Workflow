# Engage — Making the Best Action Choices (Ch 9)

> Load for: "what should I do right now?", priorities, feeling scattered, "am I working on the right things?", reviewing your life at higher horizons.

Allen gives **three models**, presented bottom-up (most mundane first).

## 1. The Four-Criteria Model — choosing what to do *in the moment*
You pick your next action against four criteria, **in this order**:
1. **Context** — *First* filter: *"what could you possibly do, where you are, with the tools you have?"* You can only make calls where you have a phone, run errands when you're out. This is why Next Actions are sorted by **@context** (`03`) — so you only see do-able options.
2. **Time available** — how long until your next commitment. Ten minutes before a meeting → find a ten-minute action. Exploit little **"weird time"** windows.
3. **Energy available** — match the task to your gas level; keep an inventory of **low-horsepower tasks** (filing, casual reading, data entry) for low-energy states. *"One of the best ways to increase your energy is to close some of your loops."*
4. **Priority** — *"Out of all my remaining options, what is the most important thing for me to do?"* — which you can only answer if you're clear about what your work *is*: *"It is impossible to feel good about your choices unless you are clear about what your work really is."*

## 2. The Threefold Model — identifying your daily work
At any moment you're doing one of three things:
1. **Doing predefined work** — working off your Next Actions lists.
2. **Doing work as it shows up** — handling things ad hoc as they arrive.
3. **Defining your work** — processing inputs (in-basket, email) into clarified actions.

**The trap:** most people over-index on **#2** (it's the path of least resistance) and let #1 and #3 slide. *"Do ad hoc work as it shows up, not because it is the path of least resistance, but because it is the thing you need to do, vis-à-vis all the rest."* You can only judge that if you've done #3 (defined your work) and keep complete, reviewed lists. The black-belt move is shifting fluidly among the three.

## 3. The Six Horizons of Focus — reviewing your own work ("altitude")
To know your true priorities, review your commitments at six altitudes — listed top-down, but **worked bottom-up**:

| Altitude | Horizon | What lives here |
|---|---|---|
| **Runway** | Current actions | The actual calls, emails, errands, to-dos (Allen: expect **≥ 50** next-actions + waiting-fors if your lists are complete) |
| **10,000 ft** | Current projects | Everything needing >1 action — your week-to-week operational world (30–100) |
| **20,000 ft** | Areas of focus & responsibility | The **"hats" you wear** — job accountabilities + personal roles (family, health, finances). Keep an **Areas of Focus** list; review every 1–3 months |
| **30,000 ft** | 1–2 year goals | Where you've decided you're going and how |
| **40,000 ft** | 3–5 year vision | Longer-horizon career/organization/life-style direction |
| **50,000+ ft** | Life — purpose & principles | Why you (and your work) exist — *"the core DNA"* |

> *Label nuance:* Allen's own labels drift between the bullet list (40k = vision, 30k = goals) and the running prose. Don't be dogmatic about the exact foot-markings; the **ladder of altitudes** is the point.

**Key insights:**
- *"If you're not totally sure what your job is, it will always feel overwhelming."* (the 20,000-ft level is the most commonly missing one — get the **Areas of Focus** list down.)
- **Bottom-up beats top-down in practice:** *"Trying to manage from the top down, when the bottom is out of control, may be the least effective approach."* Clear the runway and projects first; that frees the attention to think clearly about vision and purpose. *"Pay attention to which horizon is calling you."*

## How to apply
- **"What should I do now?"** → walk the **four criteria** against their current context/time/energy, then priority. Don't jump to priority first.
- **"I feel scattered / behind"** → check the **threefold** balance (are they all reactive #2?) and whether they've actually *defined* their work (#3 / processed to empty).
- **"Am I working on the right things?"** → run a **horizons** review; the usual gap is a missing **Areas of Focus (20k)** list. Get current at runway + projects *first*, then climb.
