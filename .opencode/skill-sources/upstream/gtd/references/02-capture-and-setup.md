# Capture & Setup (Ch 4–5)

> Load for: getting started, doing a brain-dump / mind-sweep, setting up the system, filing, "where do I even begin?"

## Getting started — time, space, tools (Ch 4)
- **Time:** set aside a real block. *"An ideal time frame for most people is two whole days, back to back."* Budget ~6 hrs to collect, ~8 hrs to process. Do it when you won't be interrupted (weekend/holiday), **not** "after hours" on reduced horsepower. *"Dedicate two days to this process, and it will be worth many times that in terms of your productivity and mental health."*
- **Space:** *"Everyone must have a physical locus of control from which to deal with everything else."* Set up identical systems at office, home, and (if possible) in transit. **Don't share your space** — *"You want to use your systems, not just think about them."*
- **Tools** (cheap is fine — *"the more 'executive' something looks, the more dysfunctional it really is"*): at least **three paper trays** (in / read-review / work-in-progress), a stack of plain paper (*one thought per sheet*), pen, **Post-its**, paper/binder clips, stapler, tape, **a label maker**, **plain manila file folders**, a calendar, and a trash can. For the list-manager itself Allen is **tool-agnostic** — paper, planner, or digital all work: *"Just go for simplicity, speed, and fun… have organizing tools that you love to use."*
- **The label maker** is *"a surprisingly critical tool"* — typeset labels *"change the nature of your files"* and make filing frictionless. Get your own.

## The filing system — the under-60-second rule
A fast, frictionless **general-reference** file is *critical*: *"If your filing system isn't fast, functional, and fun, you'll resist the whole process."*
- **One A–Z alphabetical system**, not multiple — multiple systems *"magnify geometrically the number of places something isn't."* File by topic/person/project.
- **It must take under a minute to file one item.** *"In the fire zone of real work, if it takes longer than sixty seconds to file something, you won't file, you'll stack."*
- Filing within **swivel/hand's reach**; keep ~a half-drawer of empty folders (reorder below ~100); keep drawers **< ¾ full**; prefer plain manila over color-coding (*"should just be a simple library"*).
- **Purge at least once a year** ("Dumpster Day").

## "Stuff" (the definition that governs capture)
*"Anything you have allowed into your psychological or physical world that doesn't belong where it is, but for which you haven't yet determined the desired outcome and the next action step."* Capture *before* you process — get a sense of the volume, see the end of the tunnel, and stop being *"distracted psychologically by an amorphous mass of stuff."*

## The mind-sweep — physical first, then mental (Ch 5)
**1. Physical gathering.** Sweep your environment for *"anything that doesn't belong where it is, the way it is, permanently,"* and put it in "in." Sequence: desktop → desk drawers (one at a time) → countertops → cabinets → floors/walls/shelves → equipment → other locations (car, home — *"do it everywhere"*).
- **Four things STAY in place** (no action): **Supplies, Reference material, Decoration, Equipment.**
- Too big for the tray? Write a note on a sheet to represent it (and **date it**).
- Beware the **"purge-and-organize bug"** — don't get derailed reorganizing; capture "Purge the file cabinet" as its own item and move on.

**2. Mental gathering (the head-dump).** Then empty your psychic RAM: *"write out each thought, each idea, each project or thing that has your attention, on a separate sheet of paper"* (one item per sheet — each gets processed individually later). *"Go for quantity. It's much better to overdo this process than to risk missing something."* Don't judge or organize yet.

## Incompletion Triggers (the prompt list)
Run a trigger list to surface what's hiding. **Professional:** projects started / to start; commitments to boss, peers, reports, customers; communications to make/get (calls, email, letters); writing to finish (reports, plans, presentations); meetings; read/review; financial; planning; waiting-for; admin (legal, insurance, personnel); marketing/sales; systems; professional development. **Personal:** projects; commitments to family/friends; other-org/volunteer; upcoming events (birthdays, travel, holidays); admin & financial (bills, taxes, insurance); waiting-for (orders, repairs, RSVPs); home/household (repairs, vehicle, appliances, areas to clean); computers; health; hobbies; errands; community; pets.

## Modern translation
Allen's mechanics are paper-era (label maker, manila folders, physical trays). The **principles transfer directly** to digital: "in-basket" = your inbox(es)/capture app; "one A–Z file, <60s, fun" = frictionless tags/folders in your notes/drive; "one thought per sheet" = one item per note/task. Keep the *discipline* (one trusted set of buckets, near-zero capture friction, regular emptying); the paper is optional.

## How to apply
- If the user wants to start: walk them through **capture first** (physical sweep + head-dump via triggers), with **zero filtering** — get it ALL out — then move to clarify (`03`).
- If they're overwhelmed: the fastest relief is a full **mind-sweep into one bucket**. Do that before anything else.
- Keep capture friction near zero; if a tool isn't "fast, functional, and fun," they'll resist it.
