# Clarify & Organize — Processing to Empty + the Buckets (Ch 6–7)

> Load for: processing a brain-dump/inbox, defining next actions, and setting up the lists. The operational heart of the method.

## Clarify (Process) — getting "in" to empty (Ch 6)
**Three processing rules:**
1. **Process the top item first** — everything gets processed *equally*, in order; even junk above a VIP note gets handled first.
2. **Process one item at a time.**
3. **Never put anything back into "in."** *"The first time you pick something up from your in-basket, decide what to do about it and where it goes. Never put it back in 'in.'"*

**Processing is deciding, not doing:** *"The verb 'process' does not mean 'spend time on.' It just means 'decide what the thing is and what action is required, and then dispatch it accordingly.'"* (Cherry-picking the urgent is "emergency scanning," not processing.) *"The in-basket is a processing station, not a storage bin."*

**Per-item flow:** "What is it?" → "Is it actionable?"
- **No** → Trash / Incubate (Someday-Maybe or tickler) / Reference.
- **Yes** → "What's the next action?" — the biggie. The next action must be the **absolute next physical, visible thing to do**, written as a concrete action with a verb + specifics: *"Call John re refrigerator,"* *"Email Sandra re press kits,"* *"Draft ideas re budget."* If the next move is to *decide* or *think*, that's not yet an action — *"Determine what you need to do in order to decide"* (e.g., "Call Susan for input," "Draft options"). Then:
  - **Do it** (< 2 min) — *"The two-minute rule is magic."* (Two minutes is a guideline — stretch to ~5–10 with a long window, shrink to clear fast.)
  - **Delegate it** (>2 min, not the best person) — hand off, preferred channel order **email → written note → voicemail → agenda item → talk directly**, and **record the date** on the Waiting For list.
  - **Defer it** (>2 min, yours) — onto the **Calendar** or a **Next Actions** list.

## Organize — the seven buckets (Ch 7)
> *"The categories must be kept visually, physically, and psychologically separate."* Mixing reference into to-reads makes you go numb to the stack; putting soft to-dos on the calendar makes you stop trusting it. *"All you really need is lists and folders."*

1. **Projects list** — every outcome needing **>1 action step** (completable within a year). It's *"not… your priorities but just… placeholders for all those open loops"* — *"a comprehensive index of your open loops."* You don't work *off* it; you review it ≥ weekly and ensure **every project has at least one current next action**. (Most people have **30–100** projects.) *"A complete and current 'Projects' list is the major operational tool for moving from tree-hugging to forest management."* One master list usually beats subdividing.
2. **Project Support material** — resources/notes/plans to support thinking. **Not** action reminders (stuffed folders "repel" you). Keep separate from pure reference; review in the Weekly Review.
3. **Calendar = the "hard landscape."** ONLY three things go on it: **(a) time-specific actions** (appointments), **(b) day-specific actions** (must happen that day), **(c) day-specific information.** *"The calendar should show only the 'hard landscape' around which you do the rest of your actions"* — sacred territory. **Death of the daily to-do list:** the urge to load the calendar with things you'd merely *like* to do that day → *"Resist this impulse."* They slip to the next day and poison your trust in the calendar.
4. **Next Actions lists, organized by CONTEXT** — the tool/place/person required. Common @-lists: **@Calls** (with phone numbers), **@Computer** (and an "Online" subset), **@Errands** (often split "@Errands—near me" / "@Errands—anywhere"), **@Office / @Home / @Anywhere**, **@Agendas** (one per key person and standing meeting — *"somewhere between three and fifteen"*), **Read/Review** (a physical/digital stack of >2-min things to read). Sorting by context means you only ever look at actions you can actually *do* where you are.
5. **Waiting For** — everything you're awaiting from others (delegated items, deliverables, replies). *"Always record the date you handed it off."* Review it to decide whether to nudge. *"The complete inventory of everything you care about that other people are supposed to be doing."*
6. **Reference** — no action, useful info → the under-60-second A–Z file (see `02`). General + specialized files.
7. **Someday/Maybe** — incubating possibilities (trips, books, skills, "fiscal-year" projects). *"Someday/Maybe's are not throwaway items. They may be some of the most interesting and creative things you'll ever get involved with."* **Review regularly** or the value evaporates. Special-interest sublists (books to read, places to go, gift ideas). Avoid vague "hold and review" piles — people hold but never review.

## The Tickler File / "43 Folders"
A *"three-dimensional calendar"* for **physical** future reminders — a personal post office to "mail things to your future self." **Setup: 43 folders — 31 daily (labeled 1–31) + 12 monthly.** Each day, empty the front daily folder into your in-basket, then refile it at the back (now that date next month). Holds a rolling next-31-days + next-12-months. *Digital equivalent:* a snooze/defer-to-date feature or dated reminders. Must be checked **every day** or you stop trusting it.

## Checklists
*"Recipes of potential ingredients"* — useful at all levels (a packing list, a "key job responsibilities" list, the Weekly Review itself). *"The more novel the situation, the more control is required"* (more checklists when unfamiliar). They also tell you *"what you don't need to be concerned about."*

## How to apply
- **This is the skill's flagship move:** take the user's raw "stuff" and **process each item to a clean result** — next action (physical verb) + outcome, sorted into the right bucket. Produce the organized output: Next Actions by @context, Projects (each with its next action), Waiting For (dated), Calendar items, Someday/Maybe, Reference, Trash.
- Enforce the disciplines: physical-visible next actions, the 2-minute flag, calendar = hard landscape only, buckets kept separate.
- *"It's OK to decide not to decide — as long as you have a decide-not-to-decide system"* (Someday/Maybe + tickler).
