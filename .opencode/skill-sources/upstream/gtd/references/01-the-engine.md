# The Engine — Five Stages + the Workflow Diagram (Ch 2)

> Load for: the core method, "how does GTD actually work?", and any time you process an item. This is the spine.

## The five stages of mastering workflow
> *"We (1) collect things that command our attention; (2) process what they mean and what to do about them; and (3) organize the results, which we (4) review as options for what we choose to (5) do."*

| 2007 term | Modern term | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Collect** | **Capture** | Get 100% of incompletes out of your head/world into trusted collection buckets. *"As soon as you attach a 'should,' 'need to,' or 'ought to' to an item, it becomes an incomplete."* |
| **Process** | **Clarify** | Decide item-by-item what each thing *is* and what to do about it. |
| **Organize** | **Organize** | Put the results where they belong (the eight categories). |
| **Review** | **Reflect** | Review the whole picture at appropriate intervals — anchored by the **Weekly Review**. |
| **Do** | **Engage** | Make trusted choices about what to do now. |

*"The quality of our workflow management is only as good as the weakest link in this five-phase chain."* All five must be integrated with consistent standards. **Don't blur the stages** — capturing is not clarifying; clarifying is not doing.

## THE Workflow Diagram (the decision tree — run every item through this)
```
"stuff" ──▶ IN-BASKET ──▶ "What is it?" ──▶ "Is it actionable?"
                                                   │
        ┌──────────────────── NO ─────────────────┤
        │                                          │
     TRASH        SOMEDAY/MAYBE (incubate)      REFERENCE
   (toss it)     (tickler / someday list)   (file; retrievable)
                                                   │
        └──────────────────── YES ────────────────┘
                                  │
                    "What's the NEXT ACTION?"
              (also: "What's the project / desired outcome?")
                                  │
        ┌─────────────┬───────────┴───────────┐
   < 2 MINUTES?    Am I the right person?   Defer it
        │ YES            │ NO                    │
     DO IT NOW      DELEGATE IT             ┌────┴─────┐
                  → WAITING FOR          CALENDAR   NEXT ACTIONS
                    (track + date)    (day/time-     (as soon as
                                       specific)      I can, by @context)

   Multi-step outcome  ─▶  it's a PROJECT  ─▶  Projects list (+ project plans/support)
```

### The branches, exactly
- **Is it actionable? → NO** gives three destinations: **Trash** (no longer needed), **Incubate** = Someday/Maybe or a tickler/calendar reminder ("nothing to do now, but maybe later"), **Reference** (no action, but useful info — file it).
- **→ YES**: determine two things — *"What 'project' or outcome have you committed to?"* and *"What's the next action required?"* — then choose:
  - **Do it** — *"If an action will take less than two minutes, it should be done at the moment it is defined."* (the **2-minute rule**)
  - **Delegate it** — if >2 min and you're not the right person → hand off and track on **Waiting For**.
  - **Defer it** — if >2 min and it's yours → **Calendar** (if day/time-specific) or a **Next Actions** list.

## The two clarifying questions (the heart of the method)
1. **"What's the next action?"** — *"the next physical, visible activity that needs to be engaged in, in order to move the current reality toward completion."* It must be the **next physical, visible behavior, without exception, on every open loop.** "Set meeting" is *not* a next action (not physical) — "Call John re refrigerator" / "Draft thoughts for the budget agenda" is.
2. **"What's the desired outcome?"** — what "done" looks like; the project's *"stake in the ground."*

## Projects
> *"I define a project as any desired result that requires more than one action step"* (and, from Allen's fuller definition, that can be completed within a year). Even small things qualify ("Get car inspected"). **You can't *do* a project:** *"You don't actually do a project; you can only do action steps related to it."* Projects live as an index on the **Projects list**; details live in **Project Support** material (see `03`).

## Collection — tools + the three success factors
**Buckets** (versions of an in-basket): physical in-tray, paper pads, digital notes, voice capture, email. **The three collection success factors:**
1. **Every open loop must be captured** and out of your head.
2. **As few buckets as you can get by with.**
3. **Empty them regularly** — and emptying ≠ finishing: *"decide what it is and what should be done with it, and, if it's still unfinished, organize it into your system. You don't put it back into 'in'!"*

## The eight organizing categories (detailed in `03`)
Non-actionable: **Trash**, **Incubate** (Someday/Maybe + tickler), **Reference**.
Actionable: **Projects list**, **Project support**, **Calendar**, **Next Actions**, **Waiting For**.

## How to apply
When the user hands you anything — a thought, a list, an inbox — **run each item through the diagram out loud**: What is it? → Actionable? → (next action + outcome) → 2-min / delegate / defer / it's a project. Always force the **next physical visible action**. That *is* the work of this skill.
